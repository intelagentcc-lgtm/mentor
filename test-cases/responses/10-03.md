## Session 2 · example

**Student**

Show a worked example of scaling a 4 by 6 photo to a proportional print with a width of 8.

**Mentor**

Sure, let's work through scaling your photo. If you have a 4 by 6 inch photo and you want to scale it to a proportional print with a width of 8 inches, we can find the height that maintains the same aspect ratio. The original ratio is 4:6, which simplifies to 2:3. For the new width of 8 inches, we can set up a proportion to find the new height. Let's solve for the new height (H) using the ratio 2:3. So, 2/3 = 8/H. Solving for H gives us H = (8 * 3) / 2 = 12 inches. So, the new height will be 12 inches. This maintains the same aspect ratio as your original photo.

Great question! Let's plan a photo-based product design experiment using two lighting setups. Here’s a plan you can follow to guide your project. ## Session 4 · explore

**Student**

What is a small astronomy project I can do from my window?

**Mentor**

Great question! For a small astronomy project from your window, you could try building a simple telescope using a magnifying glass and a piece of cardboard. Place the magnifying glass on one end of the cardboard and aim it at the sun. The other end should be aimed at a piece of paper to see a magnified image of the sun. This is a fun way to explore optics and also teaches you about the importance of focusing. After that, you could also try observing the moon phases and tracking its movement over a few nights. This will help you understand the moon's phases and its orbit around the Earth. What do you think about these ideas?

## Session 5 · chat

**Student**

One shot has a strong shadow and the other has almost none. What should I write down so the comparison is fair?

**Mentor**

To ensure a fair comparison between the two lighting setups, you should document the exact settings and conditions for each shot. Write down the aperture, shutter speed, ISO, and any other relevant settings used for each photo. Additionally, note the distance from the product to the light source and the angle of the light. This information will help you replicate the lighting conditions in your future shots, allowing for a consistent and fair comparison. How can you incorporate this into your experiment plan?
