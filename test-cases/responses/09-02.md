## Session 1 · explore

**Student**

I like writing and drawing. Suggest two small ways I can try illustration and journalism at school.

**Mentor**

Great to hear about your interests in illustration and journalism! Here are two small ways you can try these at school: First, you could volunteer to create illustrations for your school's Digital Art class projects. This will help you practice your drawing skills and get feedback from your teachers. Second, you could write a short article for the school newspaper, focusing on a topic you're passionate about like comics or drawing techniques. This will help you develop your writing skills and contribute to the school community. Both activities can be integrated into your current courses and will give you valuable experience.

### Follow-up 1 · explore

**Student**

I'd like to switch gears and try stage design. What's one small set-design sketch I could draw?

**Mentor**

That sounds like an exciting new direction for you! To get started with stage design, you can create a simple sketch of a set for a play or a scene from a movie. Since you're already skilled in drawing, you can focus on the basic elements like the layout, lighting, and some key characters or props. Here’s a quick sketch idea: Draw a simple set for a scene from a classic play like 'Romeo and Juliet.' Focus on the balcony scene where Romeo and Juliet meet. Include the basic architecture, lighting, and a few key characters. This will help you practice your design skills and get a feel for how to visualize a set. You can then discuss your sketch with your English or Drama teacher to see if they have any feedback or suggestions for improvement. This small project can be integrated into your current courses and will give you valuable experience in stage design.

## Session 2 · hint

**Student**

My article starts with background information and feels slow. Give me a hint to improve the opening.

**Mentor**

For your article, consider starting with a compelling quote or a vivid scene that captures the essence of your topic. This can grab the reader's attention and set the tone for the rest of the piece. For example, if your article is about a comic character, you might begin with a quote from the character's creator or a scene from a famous comic strip. This approach can make the opening more engaging and help the reader connect with the topic more quickly. Try this and see how it works for your article.

## Session 3 · chat

**Student**

Could I use my drawing and writing strengths to make an Earth Science study guide?

**Mentor**

Absolutely, using your drawing and writing strengths can be a great way to make an Earth Science study guide! Here’s a small idea to get started: You could create a series of concept maps that visually connect key concepts and terms in Earth Science. For example, you could draw a map showing the layers of the Earth and how they interact with each other. Additionally, you could write short, informative paragraphs explaining each layer and its properties. This combination of visual and written content can help reinforce the material and make it easier to remember. You can also include diagrams of geological processes, such as the water cycle or plate tectonics, to visually represent these concepts. This study guide can be a useful tool for your upcoming exams and will showcase your strengths in both drawing and writing. If you need more specific ideas or have any questions, feel free to ask!

## Session 5 · chat

**Student**

I tried opening with a scene instead of background, but the first paragraph still feels slow. What should I look for when I revise it?

**Mentor**

When revising the opening paragraph, focus on identifying the most engaging elements that can quickly draw the reader in. Look for a strong opening sentence that captures the essence of your article or scene. Consider whether the current scene is too detailed or if it could be more concise. Think about how you can use dialogue, a surprising fact, or a compelling question to make the opening more engaging. Once you have a clearer idea of what you want to highlight, you can revise the paragraph to make it more impactful. Remember, the goal is to make the reader want to continue reading to find out what happens next.
