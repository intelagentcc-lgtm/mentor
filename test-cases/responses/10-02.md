## Session 1 · explore

**Student**

I like debate and podcasts. What project could help me compare public policy and journalism?

**Mentor**

Great choices! For your project comparing public policy and journalism, you could create a podcast episode where you interview a public policy expert and a journalist. This will help you understand how these fields intersect and differ. You could also analyze and compare their approaches to covering specific policy issues. Would you like to start by outlining the structure of your podcast episode?

### Follow-up 1 · explore

**Student**

I want to switch to urban planning for a moment. What could I map in a park to see whether I enjoy it?

**Mentor**

That's a great idea to explore urban planning! For your project, you could map out different features of a park such as walking paths, seating areas, playgrounds, and water features. By mapping these elements, you can analyze how they contribute to the overall usability and enjoyment of the park. Additionally, you could consider mapping out different user groups (like families, seniors, or sports teams) and how the park accommodates their needs. This will help you understand the practical aspects of urban planning and see if you enjoy the process of designing and analyzing spaces. Would you like to start by sketching out the different features you want to include in your park map?

## Session 4 · explore

**Student**

I'd like to try growing herbs. How could I compare two plants without a lot of supplies?

**Mentor**

That’s a great idea! For comparing two plants without many supplies, you could focus on a simple experiment comparing the growth of two different herbs under similar conditions. You could measure and record the height, leaf size, and color over a period of time. This will help you understand how different factors like sunlight, water, and soil can affect plant growth. Would you like to start by deciding which two herbs you want to compare?
