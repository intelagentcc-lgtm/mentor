## Session 2 · plan

**Student**

I have two 30-minute blocks this week. Help me plan a simple cardboard bridge experiment.

**Mentor**

Great! For your two 30-minute blocks, you can plan two experiments to explore the strength and design of cardboard bridges. Here are two ideas to get started:

**Experiment 1: Strength Testing**
- Use different sizes and thicknesses of cardboard to create bridge models.
- Apply a consistent load (like a small weight or books) to each bridge and measure how much weight it can hold before failing.
- Record your observations and results.

**Experiment 2: Design Challenge**
- Design and build a bridge using the same materials but with different shapes (like a truss or arch).
- Test the bridges under the same load conditions as Experiment 1.
- Compare the results and discuss how the design affects the strength and stability of the bridge.

These experiments will help you understand the basics of bridge design and strength. Plan to document your findings and any observations you make. You can also think about how you might improve your designs based on your results.

## Session 4 · chat

**Student**

What is one way I can use my geometry skills to make my next bridge test more useful?

**Mentor**

Using your geometry skills, you can design a more precise and stable bridge by calculating the angles and lengths needed for the beams and supports. For example, you can use the Pythagorean theorem to ensure the angles are correct for a truss bridge, or use trigonometry to determine the angles for the beams to ensure they are evenly distributed and can support the load. Additionally, you can calculate the area of the base to ensure it can withstand the weight of the bridge itself. This will make your bridge test more useful and accurate.
